## 0. Reading frame

Twelve reports, ~7,200 lines. The reports were commissioned by [`research3.md`](../research3.md) and generated by Parallel AI at ultra4x fidelity. They are **inputs, not outputs**. Their purpose is to compress a large external-analysis sweep so the maintainers can decide what to ratify into repo policy via ADRs.

This synthesis does three things:

1. Names where the reports **converge** (multiple independent authors landing in the same place).
2. Names where the reports **diverge** (different authors pointing opposite ways, or assuming incompatible ground).
3. Surfaces **candidate ADRs** — decisions the cluster has chewed on enough that a short, explicit ADR conversation is now plausible.

It does **not** pick winners in the divergences. Those are decisions for the maintainers.

## 1. Convergent themes

Where three or more reports independently land on the same conclusion, and where the conclusions reinforce rather than merely restate each other.

### 1.1 Plausibility collapse is the dominant risk

Ten of twelve reports name "plausibility collapse" as the central failure mode — the moment a user detects that data, workflows, or system behavior are obviously fake, and the illusion of a real enterprise system shatters irrecoverably.

Reports naming it explicitly as the biggest risk: 01, 04, 09, 10, 11. Reports structuring their recommendations around avoiding it: 02, 03, 05, 06, 12.

The framing consistently points in the same direction: plausibility is won or lost in the *operational depth* (audit trails, queues, retries, async behavior, correlated telemetry), not the UI skin. Engineers will inspect the AWS resources; buyers will ask about compliance controls; product people will click through case timelines. Any one of those populations can collapse the illusion.

### 1.2 The dual correlation ID model

Four reports (05, 09, 10, 11) converge on the same correlation architecture: every event, log line, and audit entry carries **both** a technical `trace_id` (W3C Trace Context) **and** a business `case_id`. The `trace_id` powers distributed-trace waterfalls; the `case_id` powers per-entity timelines — the single highest-value observability view for this product.

This is the strongest cross-report technical convergence in the set.

### 1.3 CloudEvents as event envelope standard

Reports 05, 09, and 12 independently recommend the CloudEvents v1.0 specification as the event envelope, with reverse-DNS type naming (`com.jungle3.<domain>.<noun>.<verb_past_tense>`). No report recommends anything else.

### 1.4 Maker-checker as the highest-signal regulated cue

Reports 01, 10, and 11 agree: the single most recognizable signal of a regulated environment is a **visible, backend-enforced four-eyes approval workflow**. It is cheap to build, universally understood across finance/healthcare/government, and disproportionately powerful as a plausibility signal. Report 11 is explicit that UI-only enforcement is worse than no enforcement, because it will be the first thing a technical reviewer breaks.

### 1.5 The minimum viable realism numbers

Reports 01, 03, and 10 converge on a narrow band:

| Axis | Low | High | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Microservices | 4 | 7–12 | 03 says 4–7; 01 says 8–12 |
| Personas/roles | 3 | 10 | 03 says 3–5; 10 says 8–10 |
| Background jobs / async event types | 3 | 6 | 03 says 3–5; 01 says 4–6 |
| Visible stubs | 2 | 4 | 03 and 01 agree |

The band is tight enough to commit to — see candidate ADR in §4. The upper/lower spread is mostly a function of what level of realism the report was optimizing for (03 is "minimum floor," 10 is "insurance-specific full roster").

### 1.6 Declarative-first everywhere

Reports 04 (data), 06 (stubs), 10 (personas), and 12 (extension packs) all converge on the same authoring pattern: **declarative YAML/JSON artifacts** (Scenario Packs, stub mappings, persona definitions, seed data), **imperative code only when the declarative layer fails**. This is a shared architectural commitment, not four independent choices.

### 1.7 Customer-in-account is the dominant constraint

Reports 07, 08, 09, and 11 all treat "Jungl3 runs in the customer's AWS account" as a constraint that shapes every other decision: no phone-home telemetry by default, IAM-role-based support access with `ExternalId`, S3 Object Lock instead of hosted WORM, default AWS Budgets, data sovereignty as a first-class requirement. This is consistent with [ADR 0003](../../decisions/0003-installable-via-aws-marketplace.md) and should be read as the reports validating that decision.

### 1.8 Hybrid simulation: deterministic + stochastic

Reports 04, 05, and 10 independently propose the same simulation architecture: **Step Functions** (or equivalent) for scripted, reproducible "golden path" narratives used in demos and tests, **plus** an **EventBridge + Lambda** background engine that produces stochastic activity seeded by a canonical `run_seed` for reproducibility. Neither alone is sufficient; together they produce a system that is both demo-able and alive.

## 2. Divergences and tensions

Where the reports point different ways, or assume incompatible ground. These are the real decisions.

### 2.1 v1 domain anchor: chargebacks vs. insurance

**This is the sharpest tension in the set.**

Report 02 explicitly recommends **e-commerce chargeback / dispute handling** as the v1 domain, on the grounds that it preserves the regulated feel of insurance while using lower-risk data and broader-familiarity integrations.

Reports 10 (personas), 11 (regulated cues), and parts of 04 (synthetic data) assume **insurance claims proper** throughout — role taxonomy (Intake, Triage, Adjuster, Senior Adjuster, Approver, SIU/Fraud), data model (FNOL, policies, estimates, 837/835 EDI), and example flows are all insurance-native.

These two framings are not cosmetically different. They select for:

- Different persona taxonomies (adjuster vs. merchant/cardholder/issuer).
- Different external integrations (vendor repair shops + medical providers vs. payment networks + merchant portals).
- Different regulatory tone (state insurance commissioners + NAIC vs. card-network rules + Reg Z/Reg E).
- Different document types (estimates, police reports, medical records vs. receipts, chargeback reason codes, merchant responses).

This is open decision #19 in [CLAUDE.md §11](../../../CLAUDE.md). It is the first candidate ADR in §4. **The synthesis deliberately does not pick one.**

### 2.2 Custom admin UI scope

Report 09 argues **build the custom admin surface** — it is not bloat, it is the product. Three views (case timeline, queue health, failure/replay) are mandatory for v1.

Report 08 argues **beware the cost** of every persistent service — OpenSearch alone adds ~$70/month at idle, the admin UI compute adds another idle floor, and CloudWatch Logs ingestion for the admin UI's own telemetry compounds fast.

Report 02's proposed "Admin and observability service" overlaps with the Agent Layer inspection specialists described in [`docs/architecture/agent-layer.md`](../../architecture/agent-layer.md). This is open decision #18 in CLAUDE.md §11.

These are three separate axes of tension:

1. **Is the custom admin UI in v1 scope?** (09 says yes; 08 imposes a cost constraint; no one says no outright.)
2. **What is its idle cost profile?** (09 doesn't cost it; 08 says every idle service matters.)
3. **Is it the same surface as the Agent Layer, or distinct?** (Unresolved; neither report addresses this.)

### 2.3 Observability backend: CloudWatch-only vs. OpenSearch vs. SaaS

Report 09 recommends an **OpenTelemetry Collector → CloudWatch/X-Ray** default with an **opt-in OTLP export** path to SaaS (Grafana Cloud, Honeycomb) or self-managed stacks.

Report 11 recommends **OpenSearch** as the search/analytics layer for the audit trail, with Athena over S3/Parquet for deep history.

Report 08 warns that **OpenSearch's idle cost alone can exceed the rest of the Default-Realism profile** and proposes deferring it until a customer explicitly opts in, using CloudWatch Logs Insights until then.

Net effect: the reports individually make reasonable choices, but *in aggregate* they would stack a CloudWatch path + an OpenSearch path + an optional OTLP path into the same default install. That composition is neither costed nor recommended by any single report.

### 2.4 LLM usage scope

Report 04 (synthetic data) places LLMs inside a hardened gateway for generating free-text notes, emails, and descriptions — with mandatory PII scrubbing, cost budgets, and circuit breakers.

Report 10 (personas) uses LLMs for in-character writing-style generation per persona.

Report 11 (regulated cues) is silent on LLMs but warns against any content that could imply automated compliance attestation.

Report 12 (extension model) treats LLM-driven text as a "realism lever" to be dialed up, not a default.

No report recommends LLMs for anything load-bearing (decisions, policy determinations, routing). The scope is consistently narrow — templated text generation behind a gateway — but the reports differ on how prominent that capability should be in v1 vs. deferred.

### 2.5 Microservice upper bound: 7 vs. 12

Report 03 says 4–7 microservices is the floor and the ceiling for v1 ("above 7 adds complexity without more plausibility").

Report 01 says 8–12 microservices is the believable threshold.

Report 02's §2 (the [`research2-v1-service-build-plan.md`](../research2-v1-service-build-plan.md) predecessor) proposes ten services explicitly.

This is not an irreconcilable conflict — it is the same question asked at different altitudes — but it does require a choice. The tighter bound (4–7) buys cost discipline and clearer service boundaries; the looser bound (8–12) buys more inspectable surface area at the price of more idle compute and IAM roles. The answer interacts with open decision #16 in CLAUDE.md §11.

## 3. Decision surface

The structural decisions this cluster surfaces, grouped by area. This is descriptive — the flat backlog lives in [`DECISIONS.md`](./DECISIONS.md).

**Architecture**

- Minimal-core + versioned packs boundary (report 12).
- Microservice count and composition (reports 01, 03).
- Hybrid compute profile: Lambda + Fargate split (report 07).
- Hybrid simulation: Step Functions for golden paths + EventBridge for stochastic background (reports 04, 05, 10).
- Runtime isolation model: resource-name prefixing + scoped IAM per pack (report 12).

**Data**

- Event envelope: CloudEvents v1.0 + reverse-DNS type names (reports 05, 09, 12).
- Correlation IDs: dual `trace_id` (W3C) + `case_id` (business) (reports 05, 09, 10, 11).
- Scenario Pack authoring format for synthetic data (report 04).
- Idempotency: producer-generated `eventId`, consumer-side deduplication store (reports 05, 09).
- Backlog shape: NHPP arrivals + Lognormal-Pareto service times (report 10).

**Observability**

- Custom admin surface scope: which views are v1? (report 09)
- Telemetry stack: ADOT → CloudWatch/X-Ray default, opt-in OTLP (reports 09, 11).
- Audit store: DynamoDB-with-PITR or Aurora-INSERT-only (report 11).
- Search layer: CloudWatch Logs Insights vs. OpenSearch (reports 08, 09, 11).
- Telemetry to vendor: default-off, opt-in via CloudWatch OAM or subscription filter (report 09).

**Packaging and distribution**

- IaC authoring: CloudFormation primary, CDK-authored (report 07; also CLAUDE.md §11 #7).
- Installer UX: two-stage guided install (report 07).
- Marketplace listing model: single SaaS/container with in-product pack entitlements v1, standalone pack listings later (reports 07, 12).
- Pack distribution: signed bundles in S3 (or OCI), SBOM + provenance, Sigstore/Cosign signing (report 12).
- Cost profile: Demo / Default-Realism / Stress, with default AWS Budgets (report 08).

**Compliance feel**

- Maker-checker approval flow, backend-enforced (reports 01, 10, 11).
- Append-only audit trail with actor attribution and `role_at_time` (report 11).
- Tamper-evidence: per-event checksums + hash-chaining (report 11).
- Legal hold UI flag; retention presets with regulation-tooltip explanations (report 11).
- Marketing language discipline: no "compliant," "certified," "audited" (report 11).

**Personas and content**

- Role taxonomy for v1 domain (8 roles for insurance; TBD for chargebacks) (report 10).
- Pre-seeded workload portfolios per persona (report 10).
- Identity generation: census-derived names, safe-by-construction contact artifacts (reports 04, 10).
- Friction tuning: default rates with safety bounds; presets (Demo-friendly / Realistic Ops / Stress Surge / Auditor View) (report 10).

## 4. Candidate ADRs

Decisions in this cluster that feel ready enough to have an explicit ADR conversation. Each is **a candidate**, not an adoption. ADRs are the only way repo policy changes.

### Candidate ADR-A: v1 domain anchor

**Frame.** Pick the v1 domain anchor from the six candidates in [`research2-v1-service-build-plan.md`](../research2-v1-service-build-plan.md) §8, informed by the sharper framing in report 02 and the insurance-native assumptions in reports 10 and 11.

**Why now.** Reports 10 and 11 wrote an 8-role persona taxonomy, a pre-seeded workload plan, and a regulated-cues spec that *are all insurance-specific*. Report 02 explicitly recommends chargebacks. These cannot both be v1. Every other downstream ADR (persona taxonomy, scenario pack schema, stub catalog) forks on this decision.

**Open questions the ADR must answer.**
- Insurance (claims proper), chargebacks (payment disputes), or one of the other four candidates?
- If chargebacks, does reports 10 and 11's persona/regulated-cues work port cleanly, or does it need to be redone?
- Is the answer coupled to first-customer context (CLAUDE.md §11 #21)?

**Interacts with.** CLAUDE.md §11 #19, #16, #17.

---

### Candidate ADR-B: minimum viable realism threshold

**Frame.** Ratify a concrete MVR threshold as a shipping gate: the first Jungle cannot ship below this floor on any axis.

**Why now.** Reports 01, 03, and 10 converge on a narrow band (see §1.5). Picking numbers now lets subsequent scope decisions be measured against a gate rather than argued ad hoc.

**Proposed numbers (from convergent reports — the ADR must accept, adjust, or reject).**
- **4–7 microservices** with real inter-service calls.
- **8 personas** (upper end, because seeded history matters more than raw count).
- **3–5 async event types** producing visible queue activity.
- **2–4 intentionally visible stubs**.
- Inspectable audit trail, feature flags, and at least one seeded dashboard.
- Seeded data older than the install.

**Interacts with.** CLAUDE.md §11 #17, #16; Candidate ADR-A.

---

### Candidate ADR-C: event envelope and correlation-ID model

**Frame.** Adopt **CloudEvents v1.0** as the event envelope, with reverse-DNS type naming (`com.jungle3.<domain>.<noun>.<verb_past_tense>`), and the **dual `trace_id` + `case_id`** correlation model propagated through every event, log entry, and audit record.

**Why now.** This is the strongest cross-report convergence in the set (reports 05, 09, 10, 11, 12). It is also a foundational choice — changing it later is expensive. Other open decisions (audit trail shape, admin UI case timeline, per-entity observability) all assume this.

**What the ADR decides.**
- CloudEvents v1.0 as the envelope, always.
- Reverse-DNS type naming, canonical verb set (`created`, `updated`, `deleted`, `assigned`, `approved`, `rejected`, `escalated`, `completed`, `failed`).
- `trace_id` follows W3C Trace Context (`traceparent` header).
- `case_id` (or equivalent business entity ID) is mandatory on every event and log entry within a business workflow.
- Propagation mechanism for asynchronous hops (SQS message attributes, Step Functions context).

**Interacts with.** Candidate ADR-D (observability stack).

---

### Candidate ADR-D: v1 custom admin surface scope

**Frame.** Commit to building a custom, opinionated admin surface in v1, with a bounded, named view set — and an explicit idle cost target.

**Why now.** Report 09 argues building the admin UI is non-negotiable; deferring it guarantees plausibility collapse. Report 08 says every idle service has a cost floor. Open decision #18 in CLAUDE.md §11 notes overlap with the Agent Layer. A scoping ADR resolves all three.

**What the ADR decides.**
- Which views are in v1. Candidate set from report 09: (1) per-entity (case) timeline, (2) queue health with business framing, (3) failure inspection with one-click replay.
- Whether the admin surface is the same surface as the Agent Layer inspection specialists, a complementary layer, or distinct.
- Idle cost budget for the admin surface (interacts with ADR-F).
- Build vs. link policy: what's built in-product vs. deep-linked to AWS consoles or vendors.

**Interacts with.** CLAUDE.md §11 #18, #11; Candidate ADR-C; Candidate ADR-F.

---

### Candidate ADR-E: extension architecture (minimal core + versioned packs)

**Frame.** Commit to the **minimal core + versioned domain packs** architecture now, even though v1 ships with one pack.

**Why now.** Report 12 is explicit: deferring this boundary produces a monolithic v1 where domain logic is fused to the core, and the system becomes structurally un-extensible. The cost of committing now is small (the boundary is a coding discipline); the cost of deferring is a rewrite.

**What the ADR decides.**
- What belongs in the core (deployment/lifecycle, entitlement, observability plumbing, event envelope, declarative runtimes).
- What must live in packs (entity schemas, workflow definitions, personas, UI, seed data, stub mappings).
- Pack manifest format, versioning policy (SemVer 2.0.0), compatibility range declaration.
- Runtime isolation model (resource prefixing + scoped IAM).
- Signing and verification requirements for v1 (defer full Sigstore if too heavy; commit to at least SHA-256 digest + manifest signing).

**Interacts with.** Candidate ADR-A (the v1 pack is the v1 domain); CLAUDE.md §11 #2, #3.

---

### Candidate ADR-F: v1 cost profile and default cost controls

**Frame.** Ratify a target idle cost band for the default install, and commit to shipping cost controls (AWS Budgets, alarms, log-class tiering) by default.

**Why now.** Report 08 names idle cost as an existential risk — a $400–800/month idle install makes the prototype-environment value proposition untenable. Every other ADR has a cost implication that should be measured against an agreed target.

**Proposed target (from report 08 — the ADR must accept, adjust, or reject).**
- **Default-Realism profile idle cost: $110–150/month**, steady state.
- Demo profile: $40–60/month, ephemeral.
- Stress profile: opt-in, no upper bound.
- Default in every install: AWS Budget + SNS alert, log-class tiering (Standard vs. Infrequent Access), single-AZ NAT in dev.

**Interacts with.** Candidate ADR-D; CLAUDE.md §11 #1, #11.

## 5. Deferrals and anti-recommendations

Things multiple reports explicitly warn against building in v1. Captured here so they don't drift back into scope quietly.

- **Cryptographic WORM storage / enterprise PKI / full RBAC engines** (report 11) — high cost, low plausibility ROI for v1.
- **Automated compliance attestation reports** (report 11) — legally perilous. Jungl3 is a tool, not an audit platform.
- **AI-driven root cause analysis, automated remediation playbooks** (report 09) — defer past v1.
- **Full multi-tenant hosted control plane, SaaS admin dashboard** (report 12) — contradicts the in-account deployment model.
- **Runtime plugin loader for customer-authored code** (report 12) — security and upgrade cost too high for v1.
- **Cross-domain analytics dashboard** (report 12) — premature normalization across domains not yet validated.
- **Fully autonomous LLM agents making business decisions** (reports 04, 10) — LLM scope is bounded to templated text behind a gateway.
- **Follow-the-sun multi-region persona coverage** (report 10) — single-region, business-hours personas + 24/7 synthetic intake.
